Research

Performance Enhancement of Renewable Energy Systems Using FACTS

Overview

This M.Sc. thesis investigates how Flexible AC Transmission System (FACTS) controllers, paired with advanced and automatically-tuned control units, can improve the integration of renewable energy sources into the power grid. The work combines dynamic power system simulation with optimization algorithms to evaluate grid performance under variable renewable generation.

Problem

As renewable energy penetration increases, power grids face growing challenges with voltage regulation, reactive power balance, and dynamic stability. Conventional compensation methods are often too slow or too rigid to respond effectively to the variability of renewable generation.

Solution

The research applies FACTS controllers — including D-STATCOM-based reactive power compensation — governed by advanced control units with automated tuning algorithms, to dynamically stabilize the grid as renewable output fluctuates. Models are built and validated in MATLAB/Simulink before being assessed against optimization criteria for grid stability and performance.

Results

This research is in progress as part of an ongoing M.Sc. program (Oct 2025 – Present). Quantified results and simulation findings will be published here as the thesis is completed and defended.

Lessons Learned

  • Grid-stability research requires balancing model fidelity against computational cost when running iterative optimization routines.
  • Automated control tuning significantly reduces manual calibration effort compared to fixed-parameter FACTS control schemes.
