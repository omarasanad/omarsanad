# SIBO: fact check and wording decisions

- **The IBS link:** IBS is a real, common diagnosis. SIBO is presented as *one* possible contributor when symptoms persist, not as a replacement diagnosis.
- **Prevalence:** the share of IBS patients with SIBO varies hugely by test. Pooled estimates are about 54% with lactulose breath tests, 31% with glucose breath tests and 4% with jejunal aspirate culture (Ford et al., 2009). A 2020 meta-analysis found 35.5% in IBS against 29.7% in controls on breath tests. The script therefore gives **no percentage**.
- **Normal flora:** the small intestine is *not* sterile; it has relatively few bacteria compared with the colon.
- **Mechanism:** the extra bacteria ferment food before it is absorbed, which produces gas and causes bloating and pain. Malabsorption can lead to B12 deficiency, among others.
- **History:**
  - Metchnikoff (early 1900s) was interested in gut bacteria and "autointoxication". The video names his interest only, with no claims about his findings.
  - Blind-loop syndrome followed some gastric surgeries; bacteria overgrow in the dead-end loop, causing B12 deficiency.
  - Pimentel published studies around 2000 linking SIBO to IBS symptoms, and he co-authored the 2020 ACG SIBO guideline. The topic is still debated.
- **Diagnosis:** breath testing (glucose or lactulose; hydrogen and methane) is indirect and has limits. The lactulose test is criticised for over-diagnosis, and agreement with aspirate culture is poor. A duodenal/jejunal aspirate with ≥10³ CFU/mL is the reference standard (ACG 2020). The video shows no numbers and says the doctor interprets the result with the clinical picture.
- **Treatment:** clinician-directed treatment of the overgrowth, dietary adjustments and treatment of the underlying cause, plus follow-up because recurrence is possible. No drug names, doses or "antibiotics killing all bacteria" are shown.

Sources: ACG Clinical Guideline: SIBO (Pimentel et al., Am J Gastroenterol 2020;115:165-178); Ford et al., Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol 2009 (meta-analysis); a 2020 meta-analysis of SIBO prevalence in IBS; a 2020 comparison of lactulose breath tests with small-bowel aspirates (PubMed 32681227).
