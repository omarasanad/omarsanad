# Fact check and wording decisions

## History: Willem-Karel Dicke and the Dutch Hunger Winter
- **Claim checked:** "Dicke discovered gluten during the 1944 famine."
- **What the sources say:** Dicke (a Dutch paediatrician in The Hague) already suspected bread and wheat in the 1930s. Patients reported worse symptoms after bread or biscuits, and by 1941 he was recommending wheat-free diets.
- **The Hunger Winter (1944–45):** bread was scarce, and his celiac patients improved. When bread returned after liberation, they relapsed. His later research (thesis, 1950, with van de Kamer and Weijers) showed that wheat protein (the gliadin fraction) was responsible, and wheat- and rye-free diets then cut mortality dramatically.
- **Script wording:** "كان شاكك في العيش من قبل الحرب… وفي شتا المجاعة… لاحظ… ولما العيش رجع، رجعلهم التعب… وأبحاثه بعد كده أثبتت إن بروتين القمح هو السبب."
- **On screen:** the scene is labelled «إعادة تمثيل تعليمية» (educational reconstruction). It shows no quotations, real faces or archival claims. Signs use generic Dutch words (KINDERZIEKENHUIS, BAKKERIJ), and the people are invented.

## Medical points
- **What celiac is:** an immune-mediated disease triggered by gluten (wheat, barley, rye) that damages the small-intestinal lining (villous atrophy). It differs from wheat allergy, which is an allergic reaction, and from intolerance or non-celiac gluten sensitivity, which cause discomfort without that intestinal damage. The comparison cards show this.
- **Symptoms vary:** bloating and diarrhoea, iron-deficiency anaemia, fatigue, low calcium and vitamin D affecting bone, and dermatitis herpetiformis (an itchy rash). Some people have no clear symptoms. Symptoms alone don't make the diagnosis.
- **Diagnosis:** antibody blood tests (tTG-IgA together with total IgA) taken while the patient is still eating gluten. Hence the line "don't stop bread before the test without asking your doctor". The diagnosis is usually confirmed by endoscopy with duodenal biopsies examined under the microscope. Some paediatric pathways allow diagnosis without a biopsy at very high antibody levels, which is why the script says «غالبًا».
- **Treatment:** a strict, lifelong gluten-free diet after a confirmed diagnosis, with doctor and dietitian follow-up. Rice, corn and potatoes are naturally gluten-free. Oats only when certified uncontaminated (and some patients still don't tolerate them).
- **Recovery:** the intestine heals gradually. The video shows the villi regrowing "with time" and gives no promised timeline.

## Sources
- Clinical and Translational Science (Yan & Holt, 2009): Dicke's observations; mortality before and after the wheat/rye-free diet.
- Discover Magazine, "The grim origins of gluten-free"; Atlas Obscura, history of celiac disease; Today I Found Out. These are popular accounts of the Hunger Winter observation and the pre-war suspicion.
- ARUP Consult, Mayo Clinic Labs and AAFP: serology must be done on a gluten-containing diet; order total IgA with tTG-IgA; biopsy confirmation and the ESPGHAN no-biopsy criteria.
